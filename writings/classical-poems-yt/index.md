## Why I started the channel

For decades, I have wanted to edit an anthology of poems I love. A collection I
could pick up and enjoy without having to leaf through pages of poems that did
little for me.

I found that frustrating about anthologies. There would be poems I wanted to
return to, scattered among many that I did not appreciate much. Someone else's
selection, however thoughtful, could never quite be mine. I wanted to bring my
favourites together in one place, chosen for the pleasure I took in reading
them.

That desire stayed with me for years. It has now found a form I had not
originally imagined: [my Classic Poems YouTube
channel](https://www.youtube.com/@ClassicPoemsWiz).

## From the page to video

When I read and assimilate a poem, I visualize it. The words suggest an
atmosphere, a movement, a scene. I linger over a line, hear its rhythm, and let
an image take shape. That has always been part of how I read poetry.

Discovering what AI models could do made me think about sharing some of that
experience. I could give a poem a voice and a visual treatment, turning the
reading into a video. The anthology I had wanted to edit could become a
collection people could watch and listen to.

YouTube made that idea especially appealing. A printed anthology would have
asked someone to find the book and open it. A video could reach someone who
had never gone looking for poetry at all. They might arrive for a few minutes,
hear a poem, and find something they wanted to return to.

The selection remains personal. These are poems I love, narrated by an AI
voice whose delivery I guide. I shape the visuals around how I experience
each poem, and decide whether the result feels right.

## Bringing the poems to life

I built a tool called **poemvid** to help turn those choices into finished
videos. There is quite a bit happening behind the scenes, but the broad
process is straightforward:

1. **Choose and prepare the poem.** I start with a poem I want in my collection,
   keeping its wording, line breaks, and stanza structure intact, along with
   its title and author.
2. **Shape the reading.** I choose an AI voice and direct the delivery to suit the
   poem. The pace, pauses, and expression matter: the same words can feel very
   different depending on how they are spoken.
3. **Give it a visual treatment.** I choose colours, atmosphere, and movement
   that reflect how I experience the poem. The words remain on screen, with
   highlighting that helps the reader follow the voice.
4. **Check the narration and timing.** The reading is checked against the text,
   and the displayed words are checked for agreement with the spoken delivery.
   A convincing voice is of little use if it reads a word incorrectly or the
   visuals race ahead of it.
5. **Render, review, and share.** I bring the reading and visuals together,
   watch and listen to the result, make adjustments where needed, and upload
   the finished video to the channel.

The part I care about most is still the encounter with the poem. The production
process needs to support that: make the words easy to follow, give the reading
room to breathe, and create an atmosphere in which I would want to listen.

My old wish to edit an anthology is taking shape, one poem at a time. Each video
is a selection from that collection, accompanied by something of the way I
hear and visualize it.

If that appeals to you, [visit Classic Poems on
YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@ClassicPoemsWiz). I hope you find a poem there
that you will want to return to.
